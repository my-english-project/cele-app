01

Equal Comparisons

As … As

We use as + adjective/adverb + as to compare two things or people that are equal in some way.

Equal Comparison
as + adjective / adverb + as
Use this structure when the two things being compared are equal in some quality.
"The world's biggest bull is as big as a small elephant."
"The weather this summer is as bad as last year."
We often use as … as with expressions of possibility or ability — especially with can / could:
  • 01"Can you come as soon as possible?"
  • 02"Go to as many places as you can."
  • 03"We got here as fast as we could."
  • 04"I can do as many press-ups as you."
  • 05"You have to unwrap it as carefully as you can. It's quite fragile."
  • 01"The world's biggest bull is as big as a small elephant."
  • 02"The weather this summer is as bad as last year. It hasn't stopped raining."
  • 03"John is as tall as Glen."
  • 04"Janet is as beautiful as Jennifer."
  • 05"You are as crazy as my sister."
  • 06"We can run as fast as they can."
02

Unequal Comparisons

Not As … As

We use not as … as (or not so … as) when the two things being compared are not equal.

Unequal Comparison
not + as + adjective + as
Not so … as is also possible but less common:
"The cycling was good but not so hard as the cross-country skiing."
We can strengthen the difference with not nearly as or soften it with not quite as:
Not quite as — small difference:
"The second race was not quite as easy as the first." (First was slightly easier.)
Not nearly as — large difference:
"These new shoes are not nearly as comfortable as my old ones." (Old shoes much more comfortable.)
  • 01"It's not as heavy as I thought it would be."
  • 02"Rory hasn't grown as tall as Tommy yet."
  • 03"She's not singing as loudly as she can."
  • 04"They didn't play as well as they usually do."
  • 05"My mum is not as strict as your mum."
  • 06"Matrix II was not as interesting as Matrix I."
03

Formation Rules

Forming Comparatives & Superlatives

The form depends on the number of syllables in the adjective. There are five patterns to learn.

Comparatives compare two things or people.
"She is taller than her husband."
Superlatives show the extreme among more than two things or people.
"Paris is the biggest city in France."
Key: Count the syllables in the adjective → apply the matching rule.
1
1 syllable ending in silent -e
Add -r for comparative, -st for superlative.
nice → nicer / nicest  ·  large → larger / largest
2
1 syllable: 1 vowel + 1 consonant
Double the final consonant and add -er / -est.
wet → wetter / wettest  ·  big → bigger / biggest  ·  hot → hotter / hottest
3
1 syllable: ends in 2+ consonants or 2+ vowels
Simply add -er / -est.
clean → cleaner / cleanest  ·  cold → colder / coldest  ·  tall → taller / tallest
4
1–2 syllables ending in -y
Change -y → -i, then add -er / -est.
happy → happier / happiest  ·  dirty → dirtier / dirtiest  ·  dry → drier / driest
5
2+ syllables (no -y)
Comparative: more + adjective + than  ·  Superlative: the most + adjective
expensive → more expensive / the most expensive  ·  beautiful → more beautiful / the most beautiful
Adjective Pattern Comparative Superlative
nice 1 syl + silent e nicer the nicest
big 1 syl, v+c double bigger the biggest
cold 1 syl, 2 consonants colder the coldest
happy 2 syl, ends in -y happier the happiest
beautiful 3 syl, no -y more beautiful the most beautiful
interesting 4 syl, no -y more interesting the most interesting
04

Exceptions

Irregular Forms

These adjectives do not follow the standard rules — they must be memorised.

Adjective Comparative Superlative
good better the best
bad worse the worst
much / many more the most
little less the least
far (distance) farther the farthest
far (extent) further the furthest
Note: Further/furthest are also used in figurative or abstract senses: "We need to discuss this further."
  • 01"This is one of the most exciting films I have ever seen." (good → best)
  • 02"The weather this summer is even worse than last summer."
  • 03"A holiday by the sea is better than a holiday in the mountains."
  • 04"Who is the richest woman on earth?"
  • 05"He was the cleverest thief of all."
  • 06"Non-smokers usually live longer than smokers."

Real-World Use

Examples in Context

Seeing both forms used together in natural sentences reinforces when to choose each one.

  • 01"Egypt is much hotter than Sweden."
  • 02"The Nile River is longer and more famous than the Thames."
  • 03"My house is bigger than yours."
  • 04"This flower is more beautiful than that one."
  • 05"Russia is far larger than the UK."
  • 06"London is busier than Glasgow."
  • 01"Everest is the highest mountain in the world."
  • 02"This is one of the most exciting films I have ever seen."
  • 03"Which is the most dangerous animal in the world?"
  • 04"He was the cleverest thief of all."
  • 05"This is the most interesting book I have ever read."
  • 01"It is strange but often a Coke is more expensive than a beer."
  • 02"A holiday by the sea is better than a holiday in the mountains."
  • 03"Russian is bigger than Italy / Italian is easier than Greek."
  • 04"Dogs are more intelligent than rabbits."
  • 05"My garden is a lot more colourful than this park."
  • 06"His house is a bit more comfortable than a hotel."
