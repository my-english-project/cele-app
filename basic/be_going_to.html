01

Present Continuous Use

Be Going to: Present Continuous

We use the present continuous form of to be + go + preposition to + noun phrase to talk about movement in relation to a place or a person in the present.

When be going to functions as a present continuous structure, it describes movement towards a place or person that is happening right now. The verb go is used as a full main verb, not as part of a future construction.
Formula: Subject + am / is / are + going + to + noun phrase
"Are you going to Maggie's party tonight?" · "I'm going to the shops."
  • 01"Are you going to Maggie's party tonight?"
  • 02"I'm going to the shops. Do you want anything?"
  • 03"She's going to the doctor this afternoon."
  • 04"They're going to the market right now."
  • 05"We're going to John's place later."
Important: In this use, go is the main verb — it describes real physical movement. This is different from the future use, where going to is an auxiliary followed by another verb (e.g. going to buy).
Present Continuous (movement): "I'm going to the shop." (movement to a place)
Future (intention): "I'm going to buy milk." (future plan — going to + verb)
02

Future Use

Be Going to: Future

We can use a present form of be + going to + the base form of a main verb to talk about the future: plans and intentions, predictions, and commands.

Future Formula
Subject + am / is / are + going to + base verb
Examples of the formula:
"I am going to buy a new car next week."
"She is going to arrive late."
"They are going to be happy to see you."
Use 1 — Plans and Intentions:
"I'm going to buy a new car next week." (speaker decided before speaking)
Use 2 — Predictions (based on evidence):
"You're not going to do all this in an hour. There's just too much to do." (speaker can see the evidence)
Use 3 — Commands (strong insistence):
"I'm telling you, you're going to do it. You don't have a choice."
SignalExamples
Nextnext week, next month, next year
Tomorrowtomorrow, tomorrow morning / afternoon / night
Soonsoon, shortly, in a few days
Thisthis weekend, this evening, this summer
Intent markersI plan to, I intend to, I've decided to
Evidence markersLook! Watch out! I can see that…
  • 01"I am going to call you tonight." (plan)
  • 02"She is going to arrive late." (prediction)
  • 03"They are going to be happy to see you." (prediction)
  • 04"Look at those clouds — it is going to rain." (evidence)
  • 05"We are going to visit my grandparents next Sunday." (plan)
  • 06"You are going to do your homework right now." (command)
03

Past Form

Was / Were Going To

We use a past form of be + going to when we talk about a plan we had that may have changed.

Was / Were going to expresses an unfulfilled intention — the speaker had a plan in the past, but now that plan may no longer be valid, or things may have changed. It implies a contrast between what was planned and what actually happened.
Formula: Subject + was / were + going to + base verb
"I was going to call you, but I forgot." · "They were going to leave early."
SentenceMeaning
"Well I am going to, yes." B definitely intends to stay — no change of mind.
"Well I was going to, yes." B intended to but may have changed their mind.
Context: A: "You're not staying out in this rain, are you?"
The difference in B's reply signals whether the intention is still active (am going to) or uncertain / cancelled (was going to).
  • 01"I was going to call you, but my phone died."
  • 02"She was going to study medicine, but she changed her mind."
  • 03"We were going to travel last summer, but couldn't."
  • 04"He was going to apologize, but he never did."
  • 05"They were going to buy a new house, but the price went up."

Sentence Structure

Affirmative Sentences

Positive statements using be going to + base verb to express future plans and intentions.

Affirmative Formula
Subject + am / is / are + going to + base verb
Contractions are common in speech:
I am → I'm  ·  He is → He's  ·  They are → They're
SubjectTo BeExample
Iam going toI am going to call you tonight.
He / She / Itis going toShe is going to arrive late.
Youare going toYou are going to love this.
Weare going toWe are going to visit them.
Theyare going toThey are going to be happy to see you.
  • 01"I am going to call you tonight."
  • 02"She is going to arrive late."
  • 03"They are going to be happy to see you."
  • 04"He is going to play football after school."
  • 05"We are going to visit the museum tomorrow."
05

Sentence Structure

Negative & Question Forms

Add not after am / is / are for negatives; invert the auxiliary and subject for questions.

Negative Formula
Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + base verb
  • 01"I am not going to call you tonight."
  • 02"She is not going to arrive late." / "She isn't going to arrive late."
  • 03"They are not going to be happy to see you." / "They aren't going to be happy."
Question Formula
Am / Is / Are + Subject + going to + base verb + ?
  • 01"Are you going to call me tonight?"
  • 02"Is she going to arrive late?"
  • 03"Are they going to be happy to see you?"
  • 04"What are you going to do after class?"
  • 05"When is he going to call?"
Rule: In short answers, use the auxiliary verb (am / is / are). Never contract a positive short answer.
QuestionYes ✅No ❌
Are you going to call?Yes, I am.No, I'm not.
Is she going to arrive late?Yes, she is.No, she isn't.
Are they going to help?Yes, they are.No, they aren't.
Mistake 1 — Forgetting "to":
She is going buy a car.
She is going to buy a car.
Mistake 2 — Using "going to go to":
I am going to go to the shop.
I am going to the shop. / I am going to visit the shop.
Mistake 3 — Wrong auxiliary in questions:
Do you going to travel?
Are you going to travel?
