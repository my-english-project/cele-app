01

Definition & Modal Verb

What is Will?

We normally use WILL to speak about the future. It is always combined with another verb and is classified as a modal verb.

WILL is the primary way English speakers refer to the future. It is a modal verb — like can, would, could, and should — and it always appears alongside a main verb in the base form.
Key idea: Unlike the Present Progressive or be going to, will is used for spontaneous decisions, general predictions not based on current evidence, offers, promises, and habits.
Formula: Subject + will + base verb (no to)
"I will go to the cinema tonight."  ·  "She will be happy with her results."
Property 1 — No change in the third person:
He/She/It will go.  (NOT wills — there is no -s in third person)
Property 2 — Always followed by the base form (no to):
She will to call you.
She will call you.
Property 3 — No auxiliary "do/does" in questions or negatives:
Does she will come?
Will she come?
  • 01"I will go to the cinema tonight."
  • 02"He will play tennis tomorrow."
  • 03"She will be happy with her exam results."
  • 04"They will take the bus to the South next week."
  • 05"We will finish the project by Friday."

Sentence Structure

Affirmative Form

Positive statements with will + base verb. No changes in any person — will stays the same for all subjects.

Affirmative Formula
Subject + will + base verb
Positive contraction: will becomes 'll joined to the subject.
I will → I'll  ·  She will → She'll  ·  They will → They'll

  • 02"You will travel around the world."

03

Sentence Structure

Negative Form

Add not after will — never after the main verb. The contraction is won't (will + not).

Negative Formula
Subject + will + not + base verb
Contraction: will not → won't
I won't · You won't · He/She/It won't · We won't · They won't
Important: NOT goes between will and the main verb — never after the main verb.
Mistake 1 — Placing NOT after the main verb:
I will be not in the office tomorrow.
I will not be in the office tomorrow.
Mistake 2 — Using "don't/doesn't" instead of won't:
She doesn't will come.
She won't come. / She will not come.
Mistake 3 — Adding -s in third person:
He wills not go.
He will not go. (will never changes)
  • 01"I will not be in the office tomorrow."
  • 02"They will not stay here." / "They won't stay here."
  • 03"You will not have any problems."
  • 04"There will not be anything left to wish for."
  • 05"Don't worry, I won't tell anyone."
04

Interrogative Form

Question Form

To form a question with will, reverse the order of the subject and will. No auxiliary "do/does" needed.

Question Formula
Will + Subject + base verb + ?
Wh- Questions: Wh-word + will + Subject + base verb + ?
"When will I see you again?"  ·  "Where will they go?"
TypeOrderExample
Affirmative Subject → WILL → Verb He will be here tomorrow.
Question WILL → Subject → Verb Will he be here tomorrow?
Rule: In short answers, use will or won't. Do not use a contraction in a positive short answer.

Will they come? — Yes, they'll.
Will they come? — Yes, they will.

  • 02"Will you tell him the truth? — Yes, I will. / No, I won't."
  • 03"Will she get angry? — Yes, she will. / No, she won't."
  • 04"When will I see you again?"
  • 05"Will you help me?"

Positive & Negative Contractions

Contractions are common in both positive and negative sentences, especially in speech and informal writing.

Won't is an irregular contraction — it does not follow the standard pattern.
will + not ≠ willn't  →  the correct contraction is won't
Usage: Won't is also used to express refusal:
"I told him to clean his room but he won't do it." (refusal)
"She won't listen to anything I say." (refusal)
In context: "Don't worry, I won't tell anyone." (= will not)
06

Uses

When to Use Will

We use will in five main circumstances — from spontaneous decisions and predictions to offers, habits, and refusals.

1
Rapid Decisions

A decision made at the moment of speaking, not planned before.

"I'll call a taxi for you."
"Which one? Um, I will have the chicken sandwich."

2
Predictions

Based on personal opinion or judgement — not necessarily on visible evidence.

"I think it will rain later."
"The President will not be re-elected."

3
Offers, Promises & Threats

Offering help, making a commitment, or issuing a warning.

"I will do my best to help you."
"You look tired — I'll finish the dishes."

4
Predictable Habits

A regular, expected behaviour that always happens.

"My daughter will fall asleep as soon as she is put to bed."
"He will give up if he starts losing."

5
Won't = Refusal

When someone refuses to do something — won't expresses unwillingness.

"She won't listen to anything I say."
"I told him to clean but he won't do it."

  • ↯"I think we'll go right now." (rapid decision)
  • ↯"I think you will find the movie interesting." (prediction)
  • ↯"I will do my best to help you." (promise)
  • ↯"He will give up if he starts losing. He always does that." (habit)
  • ↯"I told him to clean his room but he won't do it." (refusal)
  • ↯"I will have it ready by tomorrow." (promise)
