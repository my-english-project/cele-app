Slide 01 · Grammar

What is the Present Perfect?

A bridge between the past and the present — connecting an experience to right now.

The Present Perfect is used to talk about life experiences — things that happened at some point before now, without saying exactly when. The focus is on whether the event happened, not on when.
Some point in the past
The action happened — we don't know or don't care exactly when.
Connection to now
The result or experience is relevant to the present conversation.
Now (today)
We use the Present Perfect to report this experience: "I have visited Paris."
Key insight: When you use "Have you ever…?", you are asking about someone's entire life up to this moment — not just yesterday or last week.

Slide 02 · Structure

The Formula

Subject + have/has + past participle — simple but powerful.

Subject + have / has + past participle
Subject Auxiliary Past Participle Example
I / You / We / They have visited I have visited Mexico City.
He / She / It has tried She has tried sushi.
I / You / We / They 've seen They 've seen that film.
He / She / It 's been He 's been to Paris.
Have / Has + subject + (ever) + past participle + ?
  • 01Have you ever eaten escargots?
  • 02Has she ever won a competition?
  • 03Have they ever visited Japan?
  • 04Have you ever seen the Northern Lights?
Subject + have / has + never + past participle
  • 01I have never ridden a horse.
  • 02She has never been abroad.
  • 03We haven't tried Thai food yet.
  • 04He has never broken a bone.
Regular (−ed)
visit → visited
try → tried
watch → watched
travel → traveled
Irregular
see → seen
eat → eaten
go → gone / been
do → done · win → won
Tip: go vs. been
gone = still there
been = went & returned

Slide 03 · Key Words

Ever, Never & Yet

Three adverbs that give the Present Perfect its power — and each one has a specific place.

1
EVER — in questions
Place ever between the subject and the past participle. It means "at any time in your life."
Have you ever climbed a mountain? · Has he ever cooked for 20 people?
2
NEVER — in negatives
Use never instead of "not…ever." It means "at no time in your life." The sentence stays positive in form.
I have never tried skydiving. · She has never missed a deadline.
3
YET — negatives & questions
Place yet at the end of the sentence. It suggests an expected action that hasn't happened so far.
I haven't seen that film yet. · Have you finished yet?
Have you ever tried never sushi?
Have you ever tried sushi? — I have never tried sushi.

Slide 04 · In Use

Questions & Answers

How a "Have you ever…?" conversation actually flows — and how to follow up naturally.

Speaker A
Have you ever been to a live concert?
Speaker B
Yes, I have! I went to a Coldplay concert last year. It was amazing!
Speaker A
Have you ever tried bungee jumping?
Speaker B
Yes, I have. I did it in New Zealand in 2022.
Notice: After "Yes, I have," we switch to the Simple Past to give details — because now we are talking about a specific time.
Speaker A
Have you ever eaten octopus?
Speaker B
No, I haven't. I've never tried seafood at all.
Speaker A
Has she ever won a prize?
Speaker B
No, she hasn't. She has never entered a competition.
Short answers: "Yes, I have" / "No, I haven't" — never use the past participle in the short answer.
When someone says yes, follow up with Simple Past questions to get details.
Present Perfect (experience) Simple Past (details)
Have you ever been to Spain? When did you go? / Where did you stay?
Have you ever met a famous person? Who did you meet? / Where was it?
Have you ever broken a bone? Which bone did you break? / How did it happen?
Have you ever tried to learn an instrument? What did you play? / How long did you study?

Slide 05 · Common Error

Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

The most common mistake — knowing when NOT to use the Present Perfect.

Present Perfect ✓
No specific time. Focus on experience or result.

I have visited London.
(sometime in my life — when? doesn't matter)
Simple Past ✓
Specific time in the past. Finished action.

I visited London in 2019.
(we know exactly when)
I have visited London in 2019.
I visited London in 2019. (specific time → Simple Past)
✓ Present Perfect ✓ Simple Past
I've seen that film. I saw it yesterday.
She's been to Japan. She went to Japan last summer.
Have you ever eaten sushi? Did you eat sushi last night?
He's won three times. He won the final in March.
Present Perfect signals
ever · never · yet · already · just · so far · recently · in my life · before · once / twice
Simple Past signals
yesterday · last week/year · in 2020 · ago · when I was… · at 3 pm · on Monday
