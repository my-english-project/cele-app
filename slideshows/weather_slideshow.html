Why Learn Weather Vocabulary?
🌍
Weather is the #1 small talk topic in English-speaking countries. Knowing how to describe, ask about, and discuss weather conditions makes you sound natural and confident in everyday English.
🗣️ Small Talk
Weather is a safe, neutral, universal topic. British English speakers in particular use it constantly to start conversations with strangers and friends alike. Lovely day, isn't it? Dreadful weather we're having!
📡 Practical Use
You need weather vocabulary to understand forecasts, travel guides, news reports, and to make plans with others safely. The forecast says heavy rain and strong winds tonight.
🌡️ Temperature Reference
🥶Freezing — below 0°C / 32°F
🧥Cold — 0–10°C / 32–50°F
🌂Cool / Mild — 10–18°C / 50–64°F
😊Warm — 18–27°C / 64–80°F
🥵Hot / Scorching — 30°C+ / 86°F+
💡 Key Grammar Point: To describe the weather, we usually say "It is..." or use "It's + adjective".
It's sunny. · It's raining. · It's cold today. · The weather is beautiful.

Key Adjectives (sunny):

Example Sentences: What a gorgeous, sunny day — let's have a picnic! It's warm and clear with a gentle breeze. It's absolutely blazing outside — I need shade! It's a lovely, balmy evening. Perfect for a walk.
⚠️ Humid vs. Dry Heat: Humid (hot + moisture, sticky feeling) vs. Dry heat (hot without moisture, common in deserts). Both are hot but feel very different! It's so humid today — my shirt is sticking to me.

Light mist, soft atmosphere
🔑 Key Difference — Cloudy vs. Overcast:
Cloudy = many clouds BUT you can still see some patches of blue sky.
Overcast = the sky is completely covered by grey clouds — no blue visible at all. It's quite cloudy, but I don't think it will rain. It's totally overcast — you should take an umbrella.
☁️ Cloud Vocabulary:
cloudyovercastgreydullgloomydrearyleaden sky
🌫️ Fog & Mist:
Fog — thick; reduces visibility to under 1 km
Mist — thin; visibility still relatively good
Haze — caused by dust, smoke, or heat The valley was covered in a thick morning mist.

🌧️ Rainy & Wet Weather
🌧️
🌧️ Rain Intensity Scale — from lightest to heaviest:
Drizzle
Light Rain
Shower
Rain
Heavy Rain
Downpour
Torrential
🌦️ Drizzle
Very light, fine rain. You barely get wet. A light jacket is enough. It's just drizzling — no need for an umbrella.
🌧️ Shower
Brief, moderate rain. Comes and goes. Common in spring. A few showers are expected in the afternoon.
⛈️ Downpour
Very heavy rain. You get completely soaked in seconds. We got caught in a terrible downpour!
Useful Rain Verbs:
to drizzle · to rain · to pour · to bucket down · to spit (BrE) · to lash down
🗣️ Common Expressions: It's raining cats and dogs! The rain is really coming down now. We got soaked to the skin. The heavens have opened!

💨

Thunderstorm
Rain + thunder + lightning
⚡ Thunder vs. Lightning:
Lightning → the bright flash of electricity you see in the sky.
Thunder → the loud rumbling sound you hear after the lightning.
Light is faster than sound, so you always see lightning before you hear thunder! I can see lightning on the horizon — a storm is coming.
💨 Wind Scale:
calm → breath of air → breeze → gusty → strong wind → gale → storm → hurricane
Storm Vocabulary:

❄️ Cold, Frosty & Snowy Weather
❄️
🥶
Cold / Chilly
Uncomfortably low temperature
🌡️
Freezing
At or below 0°C / 32°F
❄️
Frost / Icy
Ice crystals on surfaces
🌨️
Snowy / Blizzard
Snow; blizzard = snow + gales
❄️ Cold Vocabulary Spectrum:
nippy (informal) → chilly → cold → bitter → freezing → sub-zero It's a bit nippy this morning — wear a jacket! It's absolutely bitter out there — don't go without a coat.
🌨️ Snow Vocabulary:
snowflake — a single ice crystal
sleet — mix of rain and snow
hail — balls of frozen ice
black ice — invisible thin ice on roads (dangerous!)
⚠️ Extreme Cold Expressions: It's absolutely freezing out there! The roads are icy — drive very carefully. There was a heavy frost last night — everything is white. A blizzard is forecast — the airport may close. The lake has frozen over. Sub-zero temperatures are expected until Friday.
