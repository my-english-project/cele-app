01 · Present Continuous Use

Be Going To: Movement

We use the present continuous of to be + go + to + noun phrase to talk about movement towards a place or person.

Present Continuous — Movement
Subject + am / is / are + going to + place / person
In this use, go is the main verb. It describes real, physical movement happening now or around now — not a future plan.

"Are you going to Maggie's party tonight?"
"I'm going to the shops. Do you want anything?"
  • 01"Are you going to Maggie's party tonight?"
  • 02"I'm going to the shops. Do you want anything?"
  • 03"She's going to the doctor this afternoon."
  • 04"They're going to the market right now."
  • 05"We're going to John's place later."
Movement vs. Future Plan:
SentenceTypeMeaning
I'm going to the shop.MovementPhysical movement now
I'm going to buy milk.Future PlanIntention about the future

02 · Future Use

Be Going To: Future

Present form of be + going to + base verb to talk about plans, predictions, and commands.

Future Formula
Subject + am / is / are + going to + base verb
Examples:
"I am going to buy a new car next week."
"She is going to arrive late."
"They are going to be happy to see you."
1 — Plans & Intentions:
"I'm going to buy a new car next week." (decided before speaking)
2 — Predictions (evidence-based):
"You're not going to do all this in an hour. There's just too much to do." (speaker can see the evidence)
3 — Commands:
"I'm telling you, you're going to do it. You don't have a choice."
  • 01"I am going to call you tonight." (plan)
  • 02"She is going to arrive late." (prediction)
  • 03"They are going to be happy to see you." (prediction)
  • 04"Look at those clouds — it is going to rain." (evidence)
  • 05"You are going to do your homework right now." (command)

03 · Past Form

Was / Were Going To

We use a past form of be + going to when we talk about a plan we had that may have changed.

Was / Were going to expresses an unfulfilled intention — the speaker had a plan before, but that plan may have changed. It implies a contrast between what was intended and what actually happened (or did not happen).
Formula: Subject + was / were + going to + base verb
"I was going to call you, but I forgot."
Context: A says: "You're not staying out in this rain, are you?"
B's ReplyMeaning
"Well I am going to, yes." B definitely intends to stay — no change of mind.
"Well I was going to, yes." B intended to but may have changed their mind.
  • 01"I was going to call you, but my phone died."
  • 02"She was going to study medicine, but changed her mind."
  • 03"We were going to travel last summer, but couldn't."
  • 04"He was going to apologize, but never did."
  • 05"They were going to buy a new house, but the price went up."

04 · Sentence Structure

Affirmative Form

Positive statements using be going to + base verb to express future plans and intentions.

Affirmative Formula
Subject + am / is / are + going to + base verb
Examples:
"I am going to call you tonight."
"She is going to arrive late."
"They are going to be happy to see you."
SubjectTo Be + Going ToExample
Iam going toI am going to call you tonight.
He / She / Itis going toShe is going to arrive late.
You / We / Theyare going toThey are going to be happy.
  • 01"I am going to call you tonight."
  • 02"She is going to arrive late."
  • 03"They are going to be happy to see you."
  • 04"He is going to play football after school."
  • 05"We are going to visit the museum tomorrow."

05 · Sentence Structure

Negative & Question Forms

Add not after am / is / are for negatives; invert the auxiliary and subject for questions.

Negative Formula
Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + base verb
  • 01"I am not going to call you tonight."
  • 02"She is not going to arrive late." / "She isn't going to arrive late."
  • 03"They are not going to be happy." / "They aren't going to be happy."
Question Formula
Am / Is / Are + Subject + going to + base verb + ?
  • 01"Are you going to call me tonight?"
  • 02"Is she going to arrive late?"
  • 03"Are they going to be happy to see you?"
  • 04"What are you going to do after class?"
Rule: Use the auxiliary verb in short answers. Never contract a positive short answer.
QuestionYes ✅No ❌
Are you going to call?Yes, I am.No, I'm not.
Is she going to arrive late?Yes, she is.No, she isn't.
Are they going to help?Yes, they are.No, they aren't.
Mistake 1 — Forgetting "to":
She is going buy a car.
She is going to buy a car.
Mistake 2 — Using "do/does" instead of be:
Do you going to travel?
Are you going to travel?
Mistake 3 — Contracting a positive short answer:
Are you going? — Yes, I'm.
Are you going? — Yes, I am.
