01 · Definition & Modal Verb

What is Will?

We normally use WILL to speak about the future. It is always combined with another verb and is classified as a modal verb.

WILL is the primary way English speakers talk about the future. As a modal verb — like can, would, could, and should — it always appears alongside a main verb in the base form.
Formula: Subject + will + base verb (no to)
"I will go to the cinema tonight."  ·  "She will be happy."
Property 1 — No -s in third person:
He/She/It will go.  (NOT "wills")
Property 2 — Base verb, no "to":
She will to call you.
She will call you.
Property 3 — No "do/does" in questions/negatives:
Does she will come?
Will she come?
  • 01"I will go to the cinema tonight."
  • 02"He will play tennis tomorrow."
  • 03"She will be happy with her exam results."
  • 04"They will take the bus to the South next week."
  • 05"We will finish the project by Friday."

02 · Sentence Structure

Affirmative Form

Positive statements: Subject + will + base verb. No changes for any person — will is invariable.

I will → I'll  ·  She will → She'll  ·  They will → They'll

03 · Sentence Structure

Negative Form

Add not after will — never after the main verb. Contraction: won't (will + not).

Negative Formula
Subject + will + not + base verb
Contraction: will not → won't
I won't · She won't · They won't
Mistake 1 — NOT after the main verb:
I will be not in the office tomorrow.
I will not be in the office tomorrow. ✓
Mistake 2 — "Don't/doesn't" instead of won't:
They doesn't will stay here.
They won't stay here. ✓
Mistake 3 — Correct contraction:
She willn't come.
She won't come. ✓ ("willn't" does not exist)
  • 01"I will not be in the office tomorrow."
  • 02"They won't stay here."
  • 03"You will not have any problems."
  • 04"There won't be anything left to wish for."
  • 05"Don't worry, I won't tell anyone."

04 · Interrogative Form

Question Form

Reverse the order of subject and will. No auxiliary "do/does" is needed.

Question Formula
Will + Subject + base verb + ?
Wh- Questions: Wh-word + will + Subject + base verb
"When will I see you again?"  ·  "Will you help me?"
TypeWord OrderExample
Affirmative Subject → WILL → Verb He will be here tomorrow.
Question WILL → Subject → Verb Will he be here tomorrow?
Rule: Use will or won't in short answers. Never contract a positive answer.
QuestionYes ✅No ❌
Will they win the cup?Yes, they will.No, they won't.
Will you tell him?Yes, I will.No, I won't.
Will she get angry?Yes, she will.No, she won't.
  • 01"Will they win the cup? — Yes, they will. / No, they won't."
  • 02"Will you tell him the truth?"
  • 03"Will she get angry?"
  • 04"When will I see you again?"
  • 05"Will this concert raise money for our club?"

Won't is an irregular contraction — it does not follow the standard pattern.
will + not ≠ willn't  →  correct: won't
Won't also expresses refusal:
"He won't clean his room."  ·  "She won't listen."
In context: "Don't worry, I won't tell anyone." (= will not)

06 · Uses

When to Use Will

We use will in five main circumstances — from spontaneous decisions to habits and refusals.

1
Rapid Decisions

"I will call a taxi for you."
"Um, I will have the chicken sandwich."

2
Predictions (opinion)

"I think it will rain later."
"I think you will find it interesting."

3
Offers, Promises & Threats

"I will do my best."
"I will have it ready by tomorrow."

4
Predictable Habits

"My daughter will fall asleep at once."
"He will give up if he starts losing."

5
Won't = Refusal

"She won't listen to me."
"He won't clean his room."
