01 · Core Idea

The Key Difference

Will is spontaneous or general. Be Going To has a connection to the present: a prior decision or visible evidence.

Will

Decision made at the moment of speaking.

General prediction based on personal opinion.

Offers, promises, threats, refusals.

Be Going To

Decision already made before speaking.

Prediction based on present evidence you can see/feel.

Something about to happen any second.

Ask yourself: Did I just decide this, or did I decide it before?
Just decided now → Will  ·  Decided earlier → Be Going To
Both are so common that native speakers mix them regularly. Even if you use the wrong one, you will still be understood. But in the specific situations on the next slides — only one is correct.
Biggest trap: Predictions. Both forms can express predictions — but Will = opinion, Going To = visible evidence right now.

02 · Will Only

When Only Will Works

Three situations where Be Going To cannot replace Will.

Case 1
Spontaneous Decision
"The phone is ringing!" → "I'll get it!"
"I'll buy one for you too."
Case 2
Offer / Promise / Threat
"I'll give you a discount if you buy now."
"I promise I will behave next time."
Case 3
Refusal (Won't)
"He won't clean his room."
"My car won't start."
  • 01"I'll buy one for you too." spontaneous
  • 02"I'll give you a discount if you buy now." offer
  • 03"I promise I will behave next time." promise
  • 04"My kids won't listen to me." refusal
  • 05"I'll take you to the movies if you'd like." offer

03 · Going To Only

When Only Going To Works

Three situations where Will cannot replace Be Going To.

Case 1
Prior Plan / Intention
"I'm going to accept the job offer." (decided before)
"They're going to retire — they already bought a beach house."
Case 2
Evidence-Based Prediction
"I think it is going to rain — I just felt a drop."
"I don't feel well — I'm going to throw up."
Case 3
Imminent Action
"Get back! The bomb is going to explode!"
"Watch out — you're going to fall!"
  • 01"I'm going to accept the job offer." prior plan
  • 02"It's going to rain — I just felt a drop." evidence
  • 03"I don't feel well. I'm going to throw up." evidence
  • 04"Get back! The bomb is going to explode!" imminent
  • 05"Watch out — you're going to drop that!" imminent + evidence

04 · Overlap

When Both Work

For general predictions and open futures (no prior plan), Will and Be Going To are interchangeable — the meaning is the same.

General predictions and open futures → both are correct.
Will

"It will rain tomorrow." ✓

"I think it will be a great party." ✓

"I'll probably watch TV tonight." ✓

Be Going To

"It is going to rain tomorrow." ✓

"I think it's going to be a great party." ✓

"I'm probably going to watch TV." ✓

The difference only matters with decisions (spontaneous vs prior) and evidence-based predictions. For everything else — relax, both work.
  • ✓"It will rain." = "It is going to rain." both OK
  • ✓"She will be a great teacher." = "She's going to be a great teacher." both OK
  • ✓"I think I'll watch TV." = "I think I'm going to watch TV." no plan → both OK
  • ✓"My team won't win." = "My team isn't going to win." general prediction → both OK

05 · Third Option

Present Continuous for the Future

Fixed, confirmed arrangements with another person — more than one person knows about it and preparation has already begun.

Going To (intention)

"I'm going to call Jim."
→ my plan, not confirmed with Jim

Pres. Continuous (arrangement)

"I'm meeting Jim at the airport."
→ Jim knows — it's confirmed

Signal: Is the other person already aware of the arrangement? → Present Continuous. Is it just your own intention? → Be Going To.
  • 01"I'm meeting Jim at the airport." — Jim knows
  • 02"I'm leaving tomorrow." — ticket booked
  • 03"We're having a staff meeting next Monday." — all staff told
  • 04"Are you having a party for your birthday?" — confirmed arrangement
  • 05"They aren't leaving until the end of next year." — fixed plan
Simple Present ≠ Present Continuous. Simple Present = timetable / routine.
Present Continuous

"We're having a staff meeting next Monday."
→ just that once

Simple Present

"We have a staff meeting every Monday."
→ regular timetable
